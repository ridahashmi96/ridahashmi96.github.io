About

Q+U is a small group working on the practical side of the post-quantum transition: not the mathematics, but the inventory, migration and governance work that every organisation has to do between now and the NIST deadlines.

Our work is open source. Tools run on your own infrastructure, with no cloud dependency, so they are usable in air-gapped and regulated environments.

Q+U stands for quantum plus you: the people who have to migrate real systems, not an abstract threat model.

Scanner

The scanner maps an organisation's cryptographic attack surface across TLS endpoints and SSH services in one command, then assesses every asset against FIPS 203, 204 and 205 and the NIST IR 8547 timeline.

DiscoverySubdomain enumeration, DNS resolution, web and SSH service detection, with CDN bypass via MX and SPF records.
ScanningOpenSSL TLS handshake analysis and SSH auditing per host: versions, cipher suites, certificate chains, key exchange groups, hybrid PQC detection.
AnalysisPer-endpoint quantum vulnerability score (key exchange, certificate signature, symmetric cipher) and CRITICAL to LOW migration tiers.
ReportingCryptographic Bill of Materials in JSON plus a Markdown summary, with data-quality flags on every result.

This is a prototype fork. Credit for the scanner engine goes to Qubitac, who presented AC Scanner at Black Hat USA 2026 Arsenal and DEF CON 34 Demo Labs. Q+U's changes are tracked in the fork's history.

Why now

NIST finalised ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA in 2024. NIST IR 8547 sets 2030 for deprecating quantum-vulnerable algorithms and 2035 for disallowing them. Data encrypted today with RSA or elliptic-curve key exchange can be stored and decrypted later, so the exposure is already live.

A scan of the top 35 Fortune 500 companies with the upstream scanner in April 2026 found most HTTPS endpoints with no post-quantum protection and no PQC-safe certificate signatures at all. The hard part is not the new algorithms. It is knowing where the old ones are.
