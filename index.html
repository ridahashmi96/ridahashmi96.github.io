The problemNo starting point

Organizations face quantum migration without a starting point, and the people living through it are the most common way in for attackers.

In our founding research, many businesses could not say which data or infrastructure mattered most to their success, so they had no basis for deciding what to protect first. Inventory tools show what is vulnerable, not what is critical.

Standard awareness training does not change the human picture. Across 19,500 employees over eight months, trained and untrained staff failed phishing tests at the same rate (UC San Diego, IEEE S&P 2025). What helped was interactive preparation delivered alongside the change itself.

Our insightWhat matters most?

The quantum migration fails before it starts, at the question every organization struggles with: what matters most?

Exposure

Transitions are peak exposure

Old and new systems run side by side, defaults reset, and ownership blurs. Attackers look for the moment a system is between states.

Timing

Your calendar is a security input

A migration step that is safe in a quiet month is risky in earnings week, quarter-close or open enrollment. Attackers aim for when people are rushed.

People

Change creates the pretext

"Your certificate expires today, renew now" is far more believable mid-migration. The people going through the change need preparation tied to it.

The platformSoftware and a playbook

Software that maps what matters and sequences the migration for least exposure, plus a services playbook that gets it deployed and prepares the people managing the change.

We automate as much as we can with AI and use hands-on services where complex networks need them.

1. Intake: one inventory

An audit captures what no scanner sees: owners, criticality, how long each kind of data must stay secret, the business calendar and the obligations from contracts, regulators and policy. Aggregated scanning brings in the tools the customer already has, public-surface, code, internal network and certificate management, and fills the gaps. All of it becomes one standard inventory, mapped for dependencies and re-scanned continuously.

2. Engine: everyone ranks, we schedule

What is riskyThe crypto inventory, system dependencies and team capacity, solved for the migration order that lowers exposure fastest. The core IP is an exposure-minimization objective function.
What mattersThe plan adjusted by what the audit captured from people: which systems the business depends on most, and when it is most exposed.
What is requiredFederal timelines, sector rules and contract terms built in as hard deadlines. The engine plans around obligations; it does not certify compliance or replace legal advice.

3. Playbook: carrying the plan through

Defined, repeatable service modules: a kickoff and critical-asset workshop, cutover readiness for each stage including the pretexts attackers will use, deployment support where automation stops, and AI-assisted documentation and reporting so each engagement takes less human time than the last.

People in the playbook

The engine is the core IP; the playbook is how we win early customers and build a data advantage. In the first design partnerships the founders personally run the kickoff and cutover sessions: they build trust, double as customer discovery, and capture each organization's context for the engine. Every session follows the playbook, so services stay repeatable and can later be delivered by MSPs and partners. Research starts small: one partner, one migration milestone, one measure such as simulated phishing failure during the cutover, run with an academic collaborator. The long-term payoff is an evidence-based standard for security awareness in the quantum age, and outcome data no scanner company has.

Build order: map and engine v1 first, founder-led sessions alongside them, then a standardized playbook partners can deliver, then a published standard.

Why nowDates attached

The migration now has dates attached, and almost no one is ready.

NIST IR 8547 proposes deprecating RSA and ECC after 2030 and disallowing them after 2035. Federal migration plans are being aligned to those dates, and federal rules flow into contractors, banks and healthcare.

The threat is already active: adversaries can harvest encrypted data today and decrypt it once quantum computers arrive, so long-lived data is exposed now. Early public feedback on IR 8547 asked for exactly what we build: granular guidance on what to migrate first.
